Content Refresh Decision Support

Author: Abhay Kumar
Lane: Content Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring
Date: August 25, 2026

Research question: Can structured content and search-performance signals provide useful directional support for prioritizing content that may warrant human review?

Abstract

Content teams can manage many pages at once, making it difficult to decide which pages deserve review first when search performance changes. This project studies that content-refresh prioritization problem using the anonymized FlyRank ML Internship dataset. A Random Forest classifier was trained on nine structured content and search-performance signals to predict trend_direction and support prioritization for human review. The model measured approximately 0.632 accuracy under the original random split, compared with approximately 0.600 for the majority-class baseline, while client-grouped validation produced a lower accuracy of approximately 0.584. The result suggests that the available signals contain useful directional information, but the lower unseen-client performance supports using the workflow as human-reviewed decision-support rather than an automated content-refresh system.

This project evaluates whether structured content and search-performance signals can provide directional decision support for prioritizing content review. A Random Forest classifier was trained using nine observable features and the target trend_direction. Under the original random split, the model measured an accuracy of approximately 0.632, compared with approximately 0.600 for the majority-class baseline. A client-grouped validation produced a lower measured accuracy of approximately 0.584, providing a more cautious view of generalization to unseen clients. The resulting workflow is therefore presented as a human-reviewed decision-support tool rather than an automatic content decision system.

1. Problem Framing

For a content team, the practical problem is not simply predicting a trend: it is deciding where limited review time should be spent. When many pages show different combinations of search demand, ranking position, CTR, content age, and update history, manually deciding what to investigate first can become difficult. This project evaluates whether those structured signals can help prioritize potential content-refresh opportunities for human review while keeping the final editorial decision with the reviewer.

The goal is to prioritize content observations that may warrant human review. The unit of analysis is an individual content observation associated with a pseudonymous client identifier.

The model produces a predicted trend category and confidence information. These outputs are converted into recommendations such as PERFORMANCE_REVIEW and MONITOR.

A reviewer can use these recommendations to investigate content and decide whether further action is appropriate. The system does not automatically rewrite, publish, or delete content.

2. Data

The analysis used the anonymized FlyRank ML Internship content-refresh dataset containing 30,000 rows and 44 columns. After selecting the modeling fields and removing incomplete modeling observations, 20,018 rows were used.

Features

  • search_volume
  • competition
  • cpc
  • word_count
  • char_count
  • content_age_days
  • days_since_last_update
  • ctr
  • avg_position

The target trend_direction was excluded from the feature set. Label-derived fields such as trend_pct were also excluded. The pseudonymous client_id was used only for grouped validation and was not used as a predictive feature.

3. Methodology

A Random Forest Classifier with 100 trees and random seed 42 was selected. Random Forest was chosen because it can model non-linear relationships and interactions among structured features.

The original Week-5 evaluation used an 80/20 stratified random split. Week 6 introduced a client-grouped validation design so that complete clients were separated between training and testing.

The grouped validation contained 23 training clients and 6 testing clients, with zero client overlap.

Under the original random split, the majority-class baseline measured approximately 0.600150 accuracy while the Random Forest measured approximately 0.632368.

The lower client-grouped result suggests that the random-split result may be optimistic when observations from the same client can occur in both training and testing data.

5. Leakage Audit

A feature-level leakage audit was performed. Most selected features were assessed as low leakage risk because they represent observable content or market signals.

ctr and avg_position were identified as potential timing risks. Their validity depends on whether their measurement window occurs before the outcome represented by the target.

This timing should be verified before any production use.

6. Interpretation

The model uses multiple observed content and performance signals to identify directional patterns. Signals such as content age, time since last update, CTR, and average position can contribute to prioritization.

The difference between the random-split and client-grouped results is an important limitation. The measured accuracy decreased from approximately 0.632 to approximately 0.584 under client-grouped validation.

These observations do not establish causal relationships. They should be interpreted as measured associations within the evaluated dataset.

Items assigned PERFORMANCE_REVIEW show observed performance signals such as lower CTR or weaker average position. A reviewer should verify these signals and inspect the content before taking action.

Monitor

Items assigned MONITOR did not meet the current rules for a higher review priority. Monitoring does not guarantee good performance; it indicates only that the current signals did not justify higher priority.

Human review and no-go cases

  • Do not automatically publish content.
  • Do not automatically delete content.
  • Do not automatically rewrite content.
  • Do not treat predictions as guaranteed outcomes.
  • Do not make high-impact editorial decisions without human review.

8. Limitations

  • The model is evaluated on an anonymized dataset.
  • Client-grouped validation produced lower accuracy than the random split.
  • CTR and average-position timing should be verified carefully.
  • The model does not establish causal effects.
  • The recommendations are directional rather than guaranteed.
  • The workflow is a research prototype, not a production decision system.
